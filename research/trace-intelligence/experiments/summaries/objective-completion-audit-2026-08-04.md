# Corporate trace-artifact learning objective audit

**Audit date:** 2026-08-04
**Branch:** `codex/trace-intelligence-academic-program`
**Interpretation:** “Supported” means the stated mechanism was exercised under
the recorded fixture and verifier. It does not mean production readiness,
causal user benefit, or enterprise-general transfer.

This audit maps the research objective to the strongest current evidence. A
claim remains open when the evidence is synthetic, public-proxy-only,
underpowered, silver-labeled, or missing a downstream outcome.

## Conclusions from the audit

1. No academic method is disproven. The negative results are bounded to named
   datasets, models, task families, and interventions.
2. The strongest architecture signal is **structured identity and governed
   composability**, not an embedding-only memory layer.
3. The most important positive result is the composable-subplan replay, but it
   is not yet large or diverse enough to justify automatic skill promotion.
4. The largest evidence gap is not storage or vector indexing. It is labeled,
   changed-system, cross-user outcome data: whether a mined artifact or skill
   improves the next task without causing wrong-system, stale, unsafe, or
   unauthorized behavior.
5. The Trace Commons export does not close the cross-user gap: session and
   project proxies are present, but principal identity is absent. Treating
   sessions as people would be an unsupported inference, not an empirical
   result.
6. The next experiment should therefore hold the database and verifier fixed,
   compare exact/structured, dense, graph/ontology, frontier, and composable
   arms on a larger family/time/project holdout, and measure both quality and
   operational cost. Adding another database before that comparison would not
   answer the unresolved research question.
